# Narration Script — Read-Aloud Version

**Credit Card Debt in India — Know Your Rights** · 13 modules · about 17 minutes total

Talk to **one person** — imagine a friend who is scared and sitting across from you. You're not reading a news report; you're calmly explaining what you know. Warm, steady, confident. Smile slightly while you talk — people can hear it.

The words below are exactly what appears in the subtitles, so please read them as written. Small natural slips are fine — the processing matches your voice to the script.

## Before you record

**Files:** record **one file per module** and name them `m01.wav`, `m02.wav` … `m13.wav`. WAV at 48 kHz or 44.1 kHz is best; a phone voice memo (m4a) also works.

**Room:** use a small room with soft furnishings, such as a bedroom with curtains, a bed and a wardrobe. Avoid bare walls and tiled rooms. Turn off fans and AC, and silence your phone.

**Mic:** keep a fist's distance (10–15 cm) from the mic, slightly to the side. On a phone, hold it at chin level, pointing at your mouth.

**Room tone:** at the **start of every file, stay silent for 3 seconds** before speaking. I use this to remove background noise.

**Mistakes:** if you stumble, **stop, stay silent for 2 seconds, and say that sentence again from the start.** Don't stop the recording; the bad take is removed automatically.

## How to make it sound like real talking

- **Questions are real questions.** When you ask "And that money?" or "Why?", let your voice go up and pause for a beat, as if waiting for an answer.
- **Short sentences get their own moment.** "Relax." "Please don't." "Never." Say them slowly, with a pause after.
- `⏸` means take a breath and pause a little longer, because a new idea starts.
- **Bold** phrases are the key messages: slow down slightly and lean on them.
- Numbers like "forty-eight percent" or "twenty-five to thirty-five percent": say them clearly, never rushed.
- Don't read in one flat tone. Go softer for the reassuring parts ("You didn't steal anything.") and firmer for warnings ("Never make cash payments to an agent.").

## Module 01 — The Core Premise

*File: `m01.wav` · De-stigmatization · about 1:53*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Hi. Let me ask you something. Is your phone ringing again and again? Forty, maybe fifty calls a day? Messages that say the police are coming, or that someone will come to your home? Maybe your family is getting calls too.

`⏸`

If yes, first, **take a deep breath.** You're not alone. This happens to lakhs of people in India.

`⏸`

It usually starts with something you didn't plan. Maybe someone got sick. Maybe you lost your job. Maybe your business slowed down. You miss one payment, and within days, it feels like everyone is after you.

`⏸`

And when we panic, we make mistakes. People take loans from apps. They sell family gold. Just to pay the 'Minimum Due'. But here's the sad part. Most of that money just goes into interest.

`⏸`

So listen to me carefully. **Debt is a financial event, not a moral crime.** You didn't steal. You didn't cheat. You just hit a money problem. That does not make you a bad person, and it's not a crime.

`⏸`

Now, let me show you something. Look at the business side. Why do credit cards charge around 42% to 48% interest every year? Because the bank took nothing from you as security. No house. No gold. No land. They know some people won't be able to pay. That big interest is how they cover that risk. They planned for it from day one.

`⏸`

And remember the Golden Rule. **Your family comes first.** Food, medicine, rent, school fees. All of that takes absolute priority over paying the bank. Never starve your family to pay a card bill. Okay? Let's fix this the right way, step by step.

## Module 02 — Legal Reality

*File: `m02.wav` · Civil breach vs. criminal law · about 1:45*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Now, what's the biggest weapon recovery agents use? Fear. They'll shout things like 'Section 420', 'criminal case', 'police will come'. Scary, right? So let's check what the law really says.

`⏸`

A credit card or a personal loan is just a contract, an agreement between you and the bank. It comes under the Indian Contract Act of 1872. If you can't pay because you really ran out of money, that's usually a civil matter. Not a criminal one.

`⏸`

So what can the bank actually do? Their options are civil ones. They can file a money case in civil court, called a summary suit under Order 37 of the CPC. They can take it to a National Lok Adalat. Or they can file a normal recovery case. But here's the most important part. **Indian criminal law has no rule to arrest you just because you honestly couldn't pay.**

`⏸`

What about cheating? The Supreme Court has said, in established judgments, that cheating means you planned to fool the bank from the very first day. If you maintained a normal payment record before your problems started, that shows you had no such plan. So a cheating case is very unlikely to stand.

`⏸`

And the police? Police are not the bank's recovery agents. An unpaid card bill is not a police matter, and police usually shouldn't call you to the station for it. You may hear about Section 138 or Section 25 PSSA. Those are for bounced cheques and failed auto-debits. Pure credit card defaults usually don't come under them.

## Module 03 — Portfolio Architecture

*File: `m03.wav` · Recovery economics · about 1:14*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Do you have cards from many banks? Then you might be scared of this. What if all the banks join together and file one big case against you?

`⏸`

**Relax.** It doesn't work like that. Every card is a separate agreement with a separate company. SBI Card cannot join with ICICI. Axis cannot share costs with Kotak. Each bank must hire its own lawyer, pay its own court fees, and fight its own case.

`⏸`

And that's expensive for them. Let me show you. Look at how recovery works for different amounts. For small amounts, under fifteen thousand, or even up to fifty thousand, banks mostly just use computer calls and call centres. Why? Filing a civil suit can cost a bank fifteen to thirty-five thousand rupees. Now think about it. Would you spend twenty-five thousand to get back fifteen thousand? No, right? Banks usually won't either.

`⏸`

Even in high tiers above one lakh, card cases rarely go to court. Courts are slow. Cases take years. That's why compromise settlements, often through Lok Adalat, are usually the easiest way out for the bank.

## Module 04 — Phantom Accounting

*File: `m04.wav` · The revolving debt trap · about 1:08*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Have you noticed this? Your card is blocked. You haven't spent even one rupee. But the amount keeps going up and up. Strange, right? I call this Phantom Accounting.

`⏸`

Here's what happens. The moment you miss payments, the bank's computer starts adding charges by itself. First, late payment charges. Then revolving finance charges, which means interest, up to forty-eight percent a year. Then over-limit charges. And here's the funny part. You only crossed your limit because of their own fees! And on top of everything, eighteen percent GST on every charge.

`⏸`

In six months, a one-lakh rupee debt can show up as one lakh sixty-five thousand. The bank knows it will never collect most of that extra.

`⏸`

**Now, good news.** Under the RBI Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs, when you do a proper One-Time Settlement, these penalties and extra interest may be waived, often completely. So always try to settle based on what you actually spent. Not the big number on your statement.

## Module 05 — Asset Defense

*File: `m05.wav` · The banker's right of set-off · about 1:07*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Before you answer even one recovery call, do this first. **Protect your family's money.** This is very important.

`⏸`

Under Section 171 of the Indian Contract Act, and the rules in most bank agreements, banks have something called the Right of Set-Off. What does that mean for you? Let me explain simply. If you have an unpaid card with Bank A, and your salary also comes into your savings account at Bank A, the bank may sweep your savings balance to zero. They don't need a court order. Imagine waking up on salary day and finding nothing in your account.

`⏸`

So here's your first move. Move your salary, your savings, and your family's money to a totally clean bank. A bank where you have no loan, no credit card, and no overdraft. For example, Bank of Baroda.

`⏸`

And keep every bank where you owe money at a zero balance. Your family's groceries, medicines, and home loan EMI always come before credit card bills.

## Module 06 — The Legal Shield

*File: `m06.wav` · PNO intimation protocol · about 1:12*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Now, many people think, 'I'll just switch off my phone and disappear.' **Please don't do that.** If you go totally silent, the bank's system may mark you as 'Skip or Absconding'. That means they think you ran away. And then they start calling your relatives, your friends, even your office.

`⏸`

**Instead, build a paper trail.** Send a proper email to the bank's Principal Nodal Officer. That's the top person who handles complaints in the bank.

`⏸`

In that email, write four simple things. First, explain what happened. Illness, job loss, whatever it was. Second, declare that you are reachable, and you're not running away. Third, request that because of the stress, they talk to you only in writing, by email. Fourth, state that you want to settle with a proper One-Time Settlement once your money situation is better.

`⏸`

The bank will reply with a reference number. Keep it safe. The Case Reference ID is your proof. It shows you acted honestly. It helps counter any claim of 'Willful Default'. And it helps you later at Lok Adalat.

## Module 07 — Recovery Counter-Tactics

*File: `m07.wav` · Doorstep rules · about 1:42*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Recovery agents often use the same few tricks to scare you. Once you know them, they stop working. Ready? Let's go one by one.

`⏸`

Number One: the WhatsApp live location message. You get a text saying, 'Our team is near your house. Share your live location now.' Now think. If they really had your address, and the right to visit, why would they need your location? They may be sitting in a call center, just trying their luck. **Just block the number.**

`⏸`

Number Two: the ghost visit SMS. 'Our agent visited your house today. The door was locked.' This is often an automated script, a message sent to thousands of people at the same time. In many cases, nobody came to your door at all.

`⏸`

Number Three: the tracking link. A message says, 'Click here to see your legal notice.' These are often ready-made messages with tracking tokens inside. If you click, they may see your IP and your phone details, and they know you're scared. Real court summons come properly, for example by registered post or through the court.

`⏸`

And if someone actually knocks on your door? **Don't let them in.** Step outside and start a video recording on your phone. Politely ask for two documents: their ID card, and a letter from the bank for your account. If they cannot produce both, remind them of the RBI Fair Practices Code. And if you ever feel unsafe, dial 112. People without proper papers usually leave quickly.

## Module 08 — Telecom Defense

*File: `m08.wav` · Technical filtering · about 0:58*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Here's a simple truth. You can't win against a computer with emotions. Most of these calls come from a computer that dials numbers automatically. Every time you answer, even just to explain or argue, the system may mark your number as 'Active'. And an active number can get even more calls the next day.

`⏸`

So what's the answer? **The answer is mechanical silence.** Split your phone lines. Keep work and family on SIM 1. On your card number, block the telemarketing series, like one-four-zero, and known call-centre numbers. And turn on 'Silence Unknown Callers' in WhatsApp. Easy, right?

`⏸`

Outside agencies usually get your file for sixty to ninety days. When they get total silence, no clicks, and no small payments, their time may run out. The calls typically slow down, and the file may go back to the bank's own team.

## Module 09 — The Delinquency Lifecycle

*File: `m09.wav` · Day 0 to day 360 · about 1:19*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Now let me tell you something most people don't know. Settlements follow the bank's own calendar. Once you understand it, you'll see **why patience matters.**

`⏸`

In the first thirty days, the bank calls your account Special Mention Account Zero. They still expect normal payment, and customer care usually can't offer any settlement.

`⏸`

Between days thirty and ninety, called SMA-1 and SMA-2, your account may go to outside recovery agencies. This is when the calls are the worst. Banks may also offer to turn your card dues into a loan with EMIs. Think carefully before you say yes. It can start a brand new loan, with brand new problems.

`⏸`

At ninety days, RBI rules say the bank must call your account a Non-Performing Asset. After that, the bank can't count your unpaid interest as income, and it must set aside its own money to cover the loss.

`⏸`

Typically after about one hundred and eighty days, banks may write off the account from their main books. The loss is already counted. So any recovery effectively becomes extra profit for them. And this is often when real settlement offers, with bigger discounts, become possible.

## Module 11 — OTS & Lok Adalat

*File: `m11.wav` · Execution · about 1:24*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Once your account is written off, there are usually two ways to settle. A National Lok Adalat, or a direct One-Time Settlement with the bank.

`⏸`

National Lok Adalat is organized regularly by the National Legal Services Authority. Think of it as a friendly legal meeting, where both sides agree on a deal. An award passed at Lok Adalat is treated like a civil court decree. It is final. It binds both sides. And nobody can appeal against it.

`⏸`

In compromise settlements, card balances have often been reported to settle at twenty-five to thirty-five percent of what you actually spent.

`⏸`

But wait. Before you pay even one rupee, check four things. First: the settlement letter must be on the bank's official letterhead, signed by a bank officer. Never accept a letter only from a recovery agency. Second: the letter must show your exact card number and the settlement amount. Third: it must clearly say that after this payment, your account is fully closed, with zero balance. Fourth: it should promise a No Dues Certificate, usually within thirty to forty-five days.

`⏸`

And when you pay, use only official bank channels. NEFT, RTGS, or the bank's own website. **Never make cash payments to an agent. Never.**

## Module 12 — CIBIL Rehabilitation

*File: `m12.wav` · The 24-month rebuild · about 1:16*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

After you settle, your CIBIL report will show 'Settled', and your score will drop for some time. But is your financial life over? No! **That's just a myth.**

`⏸`

Credit scoring models usually care most about how you behaved recently, especially the last twenty-four to thirty-six months.

`⏸`

So once your settlements are done, and you have your No Dues Certificates, start rebuilding. Here's how. Open a small fixed deposit of twenty-five to fifty thousand rupees in a clean bank, and obtain an FD-backed Secured Credit Card on it. These cards usually don't need salary proof, because your deposit is the safety.

`⏸`

Keep your monthly usage low, under fifteen to twenty percent of the limit. Use it only for small bills. And pay the full bill on time, every single month.

`⏸`

Month after month, your report fills up with on-time payments. Within eighteen to twenty-four months, this good record can beat the old bad one, and may help bring your score back toward seven-hundred-and-fifty-plus.

`⏸`

Debt is a difficult chapter, but it's not your whole story. Protect your family first. Trust the law, not fear. And settle with dignity.

## Module 13 — Emergency Action Protocol

*File: `m13.wav` · Your 8-step summary · about 1:16*

> *(3 seconds of silence)*

Okay, let's put it all together. Here's your simple eight-step plan.

`⏸`

Step One: move your salary and savings to a clean bank, and keep your other bank balances at zero.

`⏸`

Step Two: send a hardship email to the Principal Nodal Officer of each bank, and save your Case IDs.

`⏸`

Step Three: turn on Silence Unknown Callers on WhatsApp, and block the auto-dialer numbers.

`⏸`

Step Four: stop paying small amounts or Minimum Dues that only cover fees.

`⏸`

Step Five: don't argue with callers. Ask the bank to talk to you in writing.

`⏸`

Step Six: be patient. Real settlement talks usually start after the account is written off.

`⏸`

Step Seven: do a proper One-Time Settlement, often reported around twenty-five to thirty-five percent. Check the official letter, pay the bank directly, and get your No Dues Certificate.

`⏸`

And Step Eight: rebuild your credit with an FD-backed secured card, and bring your CIBIL score back toward seven-hundred-and-fifty-plus.

`⏸`

Remember, you are not alone, and you are not a criminal. **Take control today.** The law protects your dignity. And if this helped you, please share it with someone who needs it.
